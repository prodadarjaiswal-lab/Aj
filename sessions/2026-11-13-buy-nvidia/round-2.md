## Bezos

SEAT: BEZOS
I DISAGREE MOST WITH: Buffett. His argument: waiting four days costs little, the investigation can't be valued, and 20x peak EPS is no fat pitch. Rebuttal: the investigation is a judgment-based question, not a math-based one, so no spreadsheet settles it before 17 Nov, and the 17 Nov call won't settle it either (Math-based versus judgment-based, S053). A small tranche is a cheap, capped, two-way experiment (Type 1 and Type 2, S063). He is right that the pitch is not fat. I am not swinging at it, only buying a seat at the table. "Being wrong may be less costly than you think, whereas being slow is going to be expensive for sure." (S064) Slow here costs only four days, so that line argues for a small start, not a big one.
I CONCEDE: Buffett's strongest point is that waiting costs little and the first tranche buys no new information. That removes my "missed rebound" argument as a main reason. My case now rests on a small start being cheap, not on a rebound. So I cut the first tranche from one third to one quarter.
ON THE CRUXES:

2. $159.93 is not a margin of safety. Bear $135 is about 16% below. It is a bounded loss at this size, not a cushion.
3. Treat the $500bn as circular until the revenue split is shown. I assume a 10-15% cut to FY28 EPS ($12 down to about $10.50), which leaves base near $190 at 18x.
4. Gate the rest on results plus no investigation finding against the company. A named permanent CEO is a bonus, not a gate, because boards can take months and a gate I don't control means the money never goes in.

## Musk

I DISAGREE MOST WITH: Bezos. His argument: a third now is a cheap Type 2 call, and being slow costs more than being wrong. My rebuttal is that the risk dial depends on who is on board. "Starship does not have anyone on board, so we can blow things up." (S009) Here the investor's savings are aboard. A fast test is only worth running if it returns information, and buying before 17 Nov returns none. The results arrive in four days at no cost.

I CONCEDE: Buffett's point that the bear case is too kind. Half of $1.3tn is $650bn, below 2026's $800bn, so capex would fall about 19%. That puts FY28 EPS near $7-8, not $9. At 15x that is roughly $105-120, which is 25-35% below $159.93. My "$135 covers it" was wrong. It also means a third bought now loses about 10% in that case, and for no gain in knowledge.

ON THE CRUXES:
1. Buffett is right. Waiting costs four days, and the rebound I would miss is not worth the risk. The investigation cannot be valued, so I buy after the print.
2. $160 is not a margin of safety against my own assumption. It is a fair price only if capex holds. "get the estimate to the nearest order of magnitude, um, you know, closest factor of ten." (S033) The bear is $110, not $135.
3. The financing is circular until Nvidia gives a revenue split. I would cut the FY28 estimate $15.71 to roughly $12, which is the Chair's base case. That is an assumption, since the brief gives no split.
4. Yes. "if you're the cavalry captain, you should know how to ride a horse." (S015) The rest of the position waits for a technical, permanent CEO.
