# Round 1: opening positions

## Buffett

SEAT: BUFFETT (Money and capital)
STANCE: Conditional yes (on option C: wait for the results and a named permanent CEO, then decide)
OPTION SCORES: A 2/10 · B 4/10 · C 7/10 · D 5/10
PILLAR SCORE: 6/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Option C. Buy nothing before 17 Nov. Afterwards, if the price is near the $135-$160 range and the CEO and investigation picture is clear, start with a first third. Do not buy the full position now.

WHY:
1. "Fallen 30%" tells you only what Mr. Market is offering, not what the business is worth. "Mr. Market is there to serve you, not to guide you." (Mr. Market, S060) At $160.76 you pay about 20x trailing EPS of $7.97, but that EPS comes from a $96bn quarter at a 75% gross margin, which is the peak of a spending cycle. The bear case is $135 and the bull case is $314. Against the bear case you can lose about 16%. That is not a wide margin of safety. "If we calculate the value of a common stock to be only slightly higher than its price, we're not interested in buying." (Margin of safety, S065)
2. The moat is wide today, but the cash comes from five customers' capex, assumed at about $800bn in 2026 and $1.3tn in 2027. Those customers are partly funded by debt, and Nvidia is reported to be helping arrange about $500bn of customer financing. I would ask whether the birds in the bush are certain (Birds in the bush, S011). Vendor financing of your own buyers is how good businesses turn gruesome. "Financial staying power" matters here (S095). The company is not about to fail. The risk is that earnings are being pulled forward.
3. Integrity and key-person risk is now live. The CEO resigned over a reported scandal with a possible national-security angle, and the investigation is unfinished. "Lose money for the firm and I will be understanding. Lose a shred of reputation for the firm and I will be ruthless." (Newspaper test, S032) I cannot value an unfinished investigation. Waiting four days costs little. At 20x trailing I am not seeing a fat pitch (S025). This is also well outside my circle: "I don't know where the information technology businesses are going to be in 10 or 15 years." (S025)

THE RISK I SEE: Peak earnings capitalised at a fair multiple. If 2027 capex stalls, EPS of $9 at 15x gives $135, and you also pay for the CEO change and a board with no successor.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: A named permanent CEO with a clean record, the investigation closed without any finding on the company, Q3 revenue above $104bn, and guidance that supports FY2028 EPS of $12 or more. Then I would buy at or below about $150. Below $135 I would buy more.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Do not place the order. Write down your own EPS for FY2028 and your own price range, then read the 17 Nov results against those numbers, not against the headlines.

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: If this stock is at $110 in two years, can you hold it without selling, and is this money you will not need? Also, why this stock and not the index fund, which already owns it?

## Bezos

SEAT: BEZOS (Strategy and decisions)
STANCE: Conditional yes (on B)
OPTION SCORES: A 3/10 · B 7/10 · C 6/10 · D 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 7/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Option B, but shrink the first tranche: buy about one third of the intended position now, and hold the rest until the 17 Nov results are read and a CEO plan exists.

WHY:
1. Sort the door first. Buying a third is a Type 2 call. "Type 2 decisions can and should be made quickly by high judgment individuals or small groups." (S063) You are the one owner. Spending a third now costs little and gets you a position you can add to or leave. Buying all of it now is different. Scenario maths puts the bear case at $135, which is 16% below $160.76. The base case of $216 is 34% above it and the bull case of $314 is 95% above. That pays, but you would be sizing a bet before the most important data in six weeks arrives.
2. Do not wait for 90 percent, and do not guess at 40 percent either. "Being wrong may be less costly than you think, whereas being slow is going to be expensive for sure." (S064) Here, though, being slow costs four days, not a year. The real unknowns are Q3 results (consensus about $104-109bn), the first call without Huang, and the FBI inquiry. Option C is the fallback, but it risks missing a rebound of 10 to 20 percent. A small tranche protects against that.
3. Judge the cash, not the sentiment. "shares are worth only the present value of their future cash flows, not the present value of their future earnings." (S052) Free cash flow per share is $5.25, a 3.3% yield. The danger is that customers' spending falls, or the roughly $500bn of customer financing goes sour. A 30% fall alone does not make it cheap. The 106% growth is already in the 20x trailing P/E, and the stock may be cheap only if 2027 spending holds.

THE RISK I SEE: A fake two-way door. If the investor panics on a result or headline and sells into a second leg down, a reversible call becomes a permanent loss. This is why the first tranche stays small.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Toward A: results with revenue above $109bn, guidance that holds the 2027 capex story, and Kress named permanent CEO. Toward D: evidence that customer financing is failing, or a forward EPS cut below about $11 for Jan 2028, which makes the base case roughly $200 or less (assumed 18x).

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Write down the full position size, the 5 to 10 percent cap, and the exit rule before touching the app. Then buy one third, a limit order if the platform allows it, and put the remaining tranches behind the 17 Nov print.

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: If the stock falls another 25 percent after you buy, would you hold or sell, and have you written down which?

## Graham

SEAT: GRAHAM (Product and startups)
STANCE: Conditional yes (on B)
OPTION SCORES: A 3/10 · B 7/10 · C 6/10 · D 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 8/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: possible
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Option B, buy about a third now and the rest after the 17 Nov results, because on my pillar the demand is real, but the "it fell 30% so it's cheap" lean is not a demand argument.

WHY:
1. Do people want it? Yes, badly. Revenue up 106% a year on $96.2bn a quarter, 75% gross margin, and the CFO's guide of roughly $800bn cloud capex in 2026. Customers paying that much and lining up are the "would they be bummed if it went away" test, scored 5 of 5 (Make something people want, S092; scoring guide in frameworks.md, framework 1).
2. Growth is the compass, but it points at the second derivative. A business growing 100% is not the question; the question is whether growth holds. The 2027 capex number is $1.3tn, which is itself about 60% growth, and every bear target is built on that slowing (Growth as compass, S100). Demand that is real but decelerating is still demand, just not worth the same multiple.
3. Watch who is paying. Nvidia helping arrange about $500bn of customer financing is the quality flag. Demand funded by the vendor's own financing is partly demand you are renting to yourself (Playing house, S178). I am assuming the neocloud debt stress is real but a minority of revenue; the brief gives no split.
4. Huang leaving matters less to demand than to price. Customers buy the chips and the software ecosystem, not the CEO. I treat it as a short-term shock, not a demand fact, though it is a real unknown for a founder-led company (Founder mode, S030).

Numbers: trailing P/E about 20 and scenarios of $135 / $216 / $314 against $160.76. That is roughly -16% bear, +34% base, +95% bull, and the bear is survivable only at a small position size. The 30% drop alone proves nothing.

THE RISK I SEE: Customers' 2027 spend slows and the financing loop shows its stress, so the base EPS of $12 becomes the $9 bear while the P/E also compresses.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Toward A: Q3 on 17 Nov shows revenue above $109bn and guidance that holds up growth above 50% for the January 2028 year, with the neocloud financing a small share. Toward D: guidance implying 2027 growth under 30%, or a top-five customer cutting its capex plan.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Do nothing before the 17 Nov results except write down, in one line, the Q4 revenue guide that would make me add. Then buy the first third only if the guide clears it.

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: If this position fell another 30% after you bought, would you buy more, or would you be bummed you owned it?

## Musk

SEAT: MUSK (First principles and execution speed)
STANCE: Conditional yes (on B)
OPTION SCORES: A 3/10 · B 7/10 · C 6/10 · D 2/10
PILLAR SCORE: 6/10
CONFIDENCE: 3/5
RUIN RISK: none (position capped at 5-10% of savings, no borrowed money)
DOOR: two-way

THE CALL: Option B, about one third now, the rest only after 17 Nov results and a named permanent CEO.

WHY:
1. Name the limiting factor. Chips are the binding input for AI build-out over the next few years, and I have said power is the one-year limit and chips the three-to-four-year limit (limiting factor, S002). Physics is on NVDA's side. The business is real: $96.2bn a quarter, 75% gross margin, trailing P/E about 20. "Success is one of the possible outcomes." (S010) So no drastic action: D is dumb.
2. Check the number nobody owns. The $1.3tn 2027 capex figure comes from Nvidia's own CFO, a seller. "when you, when you understand the incentives, then, then you understand the behavior." (S012) About $500bn of customer financing arranged by Nvidia is demand it helps pay for. I treat that as circular until proven otherwise. "it does run ahead of the revenue right now." (S015) (Physical-unit math, S033) I assume 2027 capex lands at roughly half the claim; the Jan 2028 bear case ($9 x 15 = $135, 16% below $160.76) covers that.
3. The key-person loss is real but not fatal. Nvidia's product is the factory plus the roadmap, and Huang was chief engineer. "if you're the cavalry captain, you should know how to ride a horse." (S015) The interim CEO is the CFO. That is a hole until a technical CEO is named. A one-third start carries that risk at a size you can survive. Going full size before the first call without him is speed with no information (A).

THE RISK I SEE: The 2027 capex slowdown plus a failure in the neocloud debt. Chip demand then drops by a factor of two, not 10%. The estimates of $15.71 for FY28 get cut hard, and the stock falls to the $135 bear case or lower, with a messy CEO scandal on top.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE MY MIND: Toward A: a named technical CEO plus Q3 revenue above $109bn with FY28 guidance intact. Toward D: Q3 shows data-centre revenue growth falling below 50% a year, or financing arrangements above $500bn that Nvidia itself guarantees.

WHAT I'D DO THIS WEEK: Buy one third now. Set the rest as a plan with two gates: results on 17 Nov, and a named permanent CEO. If both gates are not met within a month, the rest does not get bought.

QUESTION FOR THE INVESTOR: If NVDA is at $110 in six months, with the CEO still not named, do you add or sit? If you cannot answer in one sentence, the position is too big.
